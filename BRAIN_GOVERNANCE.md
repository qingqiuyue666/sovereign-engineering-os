# Brain Governance

## Purpose

Define authority boundaries for humans, strategic models, engineering
models, auditor models, production models, worker models, local/private
models, tools, and automation.

## Scope

Applies to all SEIS strategy, execution, review, delivery, audit, and
market-selection work.

## Non-goals

- This file does not admit live provider execution by default.
- It does not allow model output to become fact without verification.
- It does not let low-tier models approve high-risk operations.

## Brain Tiers

| Tier | Authority | Hard Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Human Sovereign | Final authority for strategy, risk, approvals, market entry, and external claims | Cannot be bypassed by model consensus |
| Strategic Brain | Produces strategic options, market analysis, and control-point arguments | Cannot self-certify real proof |
| Engineering Brain | Designs and implements bounded technical changes | Requires review for material risk |
| Auditor Brain | Reviews claims, evidence, risk, and completion | Must be separated from executor when risk is material |
| Production Brain | Produces content, templates, artifacts, and delivery support | Cannot approve final claims |
| Worker Brain | Executes bounded low-risk tasks | No final strategic, architectural, risk, or high-stakes authority |
| Local / Private Brain | Works on sensitive local context inside boundaries | No external transmission without approval |

## Operating Rules

- Model output is a claim until verified.
- Low-tier models cannot approve strategy, high-risk operations, market
  selection, architecture, certifications, or completion.
- Executor and auditor must be separated when risk is material.
- Provider choice must remain replaceable.
- Human approval is required for high-risk execution, external claims,
  permission changes, secret exposure, or live provider admission.

## Failure Modes

- A worker model drafts a market decision and the system treats it as final.
- A model self-audits its own high-risk execution.
- A provider-specific feature becomes strategic lock-in.
- A local/private model leaks context through external tooling.

## Upgrade Path

Bind this policy to task intake, model routing, evidence checks, and review
gates after real delivery records show repeated risk patterns.
