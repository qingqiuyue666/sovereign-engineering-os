# SEIS Doctrine

## Purpose

Define the strategic doctrine for the Sovereign Economic Intelligence
System. The doctrine turns the repository away from generic AI-tool
thinking and toward economic control-point occupation.

## Scope

Applies to market selection, product strategy, delivery design, AI usage,
evidence policy, governance, proof assets, distribution, and Stage 9-16
pre-structure.

## Non-goals

- Do not claim real revenue, customer adoption, external certification, or
  Stage 16 completion without evidence.
- Do not turn this pass into broad runtime implementation.
- Do not treat model choice, agent framework, RAG, MCP, local models, or
  automation tools as the strategy.

## Core Doctrine

AI is not the strategy. AI is the engine, worker, advisor, auditor, and
tool. The strategy is to identify real economic control points and use
trusted AI/human/tool systems to occupy them.

Real economic control points matter more than features. SEIS must ask:

- who controls the budget
- what structural pressure forces action
- what buying trigger exists now
- what result can be trusted
- what process can become a delivery standard
- what data, failures, benchmarks, and proof assets compound
- what can become protocol, credit, rights, clearing, or capital allocation
- what should be rejected because it has no upward path

Stage 16 is the compass, not the current claim. The repository distinguishes
scaffold, policy, template, evidence, real market proof, real revenue, real
customer feedback, standardization, and ecosystem traction.

## Operating Rules

- Every market decision must identify buyer, budget source, trigger, trust
  gap, acceptance rule, proof speed, and upward path.
- Every delivery must produce evidence, a reusable asset, a failure record
  or benchmark, and a distribution candidate.
- Low-tier worker models may assist with bounded low-risk work but cannot
  make final strategic, risk, architecture, market-selection, or completion
  decisions.
- Trusted delivery beats raw automation: approval, audit log, evidence,
  rollback, review separation, and no-fake-success rules are mandatory.
- Claims must remain lower than evidence. A template is not proof; an
  internal report is not a customer; a demo is not revenue.

## Failure Modes

- Feature drift: the repository optimizes around tools rather than control
  points.
- Proof inflation: internal scaffolds are described as market traction.
- Automation drift: runtime capability expands faster than audit, approval,
  and rollback.
- Model authority drift: low-tier outputs become final decisions.
- Commodity drift: the offer can be replaced by a cheaper generic AI tool.

## Upgrade Path

- Add paid-signal records for the first wedge.
- Attach delivery proof, acceptance evidence, and buyer feedback.
- Convert repeated delivery patterns into standards and protocols.
- Promote only evidence-backed maturity stages.
