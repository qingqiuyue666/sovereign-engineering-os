# Rejection Rules

## Purpose

Define when SEIS should reject or defer a prospect before accepting a
readiness audit.

## Hard Reject

Reject the prospect if they require:

- uncontrolled autonomous production execution
- secret custody as part of discovery
- direct deployment authority in the entry audit
- claims of certification, compliance signoff, or guaranteed ROI
- bypassing engineering, security, or risk-owner approval
- fake case study, fake customer proof, or inflated market language
- broad SaaS/product buildout before evidence exists

## Defer

Defer when:

- no buying trigger exists
- no budget owner can be named
- no evidence can be shared
- the team only wants generic AI training
- the work is mostly code review with no governance mandate
- the buyer cannot define acceptance
- the audit would depend on legal or compliance opinions SEIS cannot provide

## Accept Only With Narrow Scope

Proceed with narrow scope when:

- there is a real trigger but evidence access is limited
- the buyer can approve a diagnostic but not a full audit
- the workflow is early but likely to reach production soon
- the buyer wants implementation but accepts audit-first boundaries

## Rejection Record

Every rejection should record:

- prospect type
- trigger status
- buyer/budget status
- evidence access status
- rejection reason
- follow-up condition
- language or objection that should update the offer

## Boundary

Rejection is a strategic control. It prevents commodity work, unsafe work,
and claims that exceed evidence.
