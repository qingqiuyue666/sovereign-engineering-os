# Target Customer Profile

## Purpose

Define who should be approached first and who should be rejected before time
is wasted.

## Best-Fit Customer

| Attribute | Target |
| --- | --- |
| Company stage | Seed to mid-market, or a business unit with direct engineering ownership. |
| Team size | 5-80 engineers, enough process to need governance but not enough bureaucracy to block change. |
| AI usage | AI-assisted code generation, review, test writing, refactoring, or workflow automation is already active. |
| Production pressure | Shipping customer-facing or internal-critical software with release risk. |
| Buyer access | Engineering or founder-level sponsor can join discovery. |
| Evidence access | Team can provide sanitized PRs, tickets, runbooks, tests, release notes, or incident examples. |
| Urgency | Trigger exists within 30-90 days. |

## Buyer Roles

- Economic buyer: CTO, founder, VP Engineering, Head of Engineering.
- Technical buyer: staff engineer, platform lead, engineering manager.
- Risk owner: security lead, compliance owner, release owner, or executive
  accountable for production quality.
- Operator: engineer or manager who can expose the actual workflow.

## Budget Control

The target must have a clear budget path. Acceptable paths:

- engineering leadership can approve directly
- founder can approve directly
- security/compliance owner funds audit readiness with engineering sponsor
- platform/productivity tooling budget covers the audit

Weak paths:

- individual contributor interest without sponsor
- innovation budget with no workflow owner
- procurement-led request with no urgency or evidence access

## Qualification Questions

- Where is AI-assisted code currently entering your workflow?
- Who is accountable if an AI-assisted change causes a production issue?
- What proof would leadership trust before expanding AI coding?
- What deadline, audit, customer pressure, or release makes this urgent?
- What evidence can you share safely during a bounded audit?

## Rejection Signals

Reject or defer prospects that:

- want uncontrolled automation instead of governance
- want secret handling or production access as a first step
- cannot name a risk owner
- have no near-term buying trigger
- only want generic AI training
- expect certification, compliance signoff, or guaranteed ROI
- cannot share enough evidence for truthful findings

## Current Evidence Status

This profile is a buyer hypothesis. It is not evidence of customer adoption,
revenue, paid demand, or market validation.
